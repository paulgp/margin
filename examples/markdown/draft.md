# The quiet interval

The library opens before the street wakes. For a few minutes, the returned books wait beside an empty desk, and nobody asks them to explain themselves.

We often describe public libraries as information services. This description leaves out the time they offer: a place to sit without buying anything, and permission to follow a question without knowing where it leads.

This is why the city should extend evening hours. Last winter, our neighborhood branch closed at six, while many residents were still on their way home. A longer day would make the library more useful.

The quiet matters too. A public room can hold a little uncertainty without immediately turning it into a task.
