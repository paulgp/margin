\section{Introduction}

A model is also an agreement about what to ignore. When two teams compare estimates, a shared vocabulary can conceal different decisions about the boundary of the system.

This is the central problem. Our study compares the error reported by three teams using the same public dataset. We argue that agreement on a dataset is enough to make their conclusions comparable.

The aim is modest: to make the assumptions visible before asking which estimate is best. Following \cite{example2024}, we report both an estimate and the conditions under which it should be read.
